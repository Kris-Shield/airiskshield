---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines how AI Risk Shield manages knowledge sources used for assessments, recommendations, and reports.

The purpose is to ensure that recommendations are:

- traceable,
- explainable,
- based on reliable information,
- resistant to unsupported AI-generated claims.

---

# 2. Core Principle

AI Risk Shield follows this rule:

> If information cannot be supported by a trusted source, the system must not present it as a fact.

The system should prefer:

"I need additional verification"

over:

"An unsupported assumption."

---

# 3. Knowledge Hierarchy

Sources are classified into levels.

---

# Level 1

## Official Regulatory Sources

Highest priority.

Examples:

- European Union regulations,
- official governmental publications,
- regulatory authority guidance.

Examples:

- EU AI Act official documentation,
- European Commission guidance,
- national data protection authority publications.

---

# Level 2

## Official Standards & Frameworks

Trusted professional frameworks.

Examples:

- ISO standards,
- NIST AI Risk Management Framework,
- recognized governance frameworks.

---

# Level 3

## Academic & Research Sources

Used for:

- technical understanding,
- AI limitations,
- risk research,
- emerging topics.

Examples:

- peer-reviewed publications,
- university research,
- recognized institutions.

---

# Level 4

## Industry Best Practices

Used for practical recommendations.

Examples:

- security practices,
- implementation guides,
- vendor documentation.

---

# Level 5

## Internal AI Risk Shield Knowledge

Created documents:

- AIRS Framework,
- scoring methodology,
- recommendation library.

---

# 4. Source Requirements

Every knowledge source should have:

- title,
- publisher,
- publication date,
- URL or reference,
- purpose,
- relevance.

---

# 5. Evidence Tagging

Each recommendation may include evidence level.

---

## Evidence A

Directly supported by official sources.

---

## Evidence B

Supported by recognized standards or professional frameworks.

---

## Evidence C

Based on industry best practice.

---

## Evidence D

Internal recommendation requiring human validation.

---

# 6. AI Response Rules

The AI system must:

## Always

- explain assumptions,
- reference available sources,
- indicate uncertainty.

---

## Never

- invent legal requirements,
- create fake article numbers,
- claim certification,
- guarantee compliance.

---

# 7. RAG Architecture Principle

AI Risk Shield uses Retrieval-Augmented Generation principles.

---

# 8. Knowledge Updates

Knowledge sources should be reviewed periodically.

Review triggers:

- regulatory changes,
- new AI standards,
- significant industry developments.

---

# 9. Human Oversight

Knowledge management requires human supervision.

AI may:

- summarize,
- organize,
- explain.

AI may not independently decide:

- what is legally required,
- what regulation means,
- whether an organization is compliant.

---

# 10. Future Development

Future versions may include:

- automated source monitoring,
- knowledge database,
- document embeddings,
- citation engine,
- source confidence scoring.
