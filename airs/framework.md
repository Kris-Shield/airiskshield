# AIRS Framework

## Artificial Intelligence Risk Standard

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Foundation Draft  
**Owner:** AI Risk Shield

---

# 1. Introduction

AIRS (Artificial Intelligence Risk Standard) is the foundational methodology behind AI Risk Shield.

AIRS provides a structured approach for evaluating how organizations use artificial intelligence and identifying opportunities to improve safety, transparency, governance, and responsible adoption.

AIRS is designed for organizations that want to benefit from AI while maintaining human responsibility and awareness of potential risks.

---

# 2. Purpose

The purpose of AIRS is to answer three fundamental questions:

## 1. How is an organization currently using AI?

Understanding:

- which AI tools are used,
- who uses them,
- for what purposes,
- what information is processed.

---

## 2. What risks or improvement opportunities exist?

Identifying areas related to:

- data protection,
- governance,
- transparency,
- human oversight,
- intellectual property,
- employee awareness.

---

## 3. What practical actions should the organization take?

Providing:

- understandable recommendations,
- prioritized improvements,
- actionable next steps.

---

# 3. Core Philosophy

AIRS is built on five principles.

---

# Principle 1

## Human Responsibility

AI systems support human decisions.

They do not replace:

- professional judgment,
- accountability,
- organizational responsibility.

---

# Principle 2

## Transparency

Organizations should understand:

- how AI is used,
- what data is involved,
- what limitations exist.

---

# Principle 3

## Evidence-Based Assessment

Risk evaluation should be based on:

- defined criteria,
- documented methodology,
- reliable sources.

---

# Principle 4

## Continuous Improvement

AI governance is not a one-time activity.

Organizations should continuously improve their practices.

---

# Principle 5

## Proportionality

Recommendations should consider:

- organization size,
- AI usage level,
- business context,
- potential impact.

Small companies should receive practical solutions, not unnecessary complexity.

---

# 4. Assessment Domains

AIRS evaluates organizations across seven primary domains.

---

# Domain 1

# AI Governance

## Purpose

Evaluate whether the organization has appropriate structures for managing AI usage.

## Assessment Areas

- AI usage policies
- internal responsibility
- approval processes
- documentation
- employee guidance

## Example Risk

Employees independently use AI tools without organizational rules.

---

# Domain 2

# Data Protection & Privacy

## Purpose

Evaluate how organizations manage information processed by AI systems.

## Assessment Areas

- personal data handling
- confidential information
- customer data
- sensitive business information
- AI provider settings

## Example Risk

Employees submit confidential company information into uncontrolled AI tools.

---

# Domain 3

# Human Oversight

## Purpose

Evaluate whether humans review and control AI-generated outputs.

## Assessment Areas

- review processes
- approval workflows
- critical decision oversight
- accountability

## Example Risk

AI-generated content is delivered to customers without human verification.

---

# Domain 4

# Transparency & Explainability

## Purpose

Evaluate whether AI usage can be understood and communicated.

## Assessment Areas

- disclosure practices
- internal awareness
- documentation
- customer communication

## Example Risk

Customers are unaware that AI significantly influences delivered services.

---

# Domain 5

# Intellectual Property

## Purpose

Evaluate ownership and usage considerations related to AI-generated content.

## Assessment Areas

- generated materials
- licensing awareness
- customer agreements
- ownership expectations

## Example Risk

Company delivers AI-generated assets without understanding ownership limitations.

---

# Domain 6

# AI Literacy

## Purpose

Evaluate whether employees understand responsible AI usage.

## Assessment Areas

- employee training
- understanding limitations
- awareness of hallucinations
- security practices

## Example Risk

Employees trust AI outputs without verification.

---

# Domain 7

# Security & Operational Controls

## Purpose

Evaluate whether AI usage is integrated safely into company operations.

## Assessment Areas

- access control
- approved tools
- account management
- monitoring practices

## Example Risk

Former employees retain access to company AI accounts.

---

# 5. Risk Assessment Philosophy

AIRS does not classify organizations as:

"good" or "bad".

AIRS identifies:

- current maturity,
- improvement opportunities,
- practical recommendations.

---

# 6. Assessment Output

The AIRS assessment produces:

## Organizational AI Profile

A summary of current AI adoption.

---

## Risk Overview

Identification of important areas requiring attention.

---

## Improvement Roadmap

Prioritized recommendations.

---

## Maturity Level

An indication of organizational AI governance maturity.

---

# 7. Maturity Model

AIRS uses five maturity levels.

---

# Level 1

## Explorer

Organization is beginning AI adoption.

Characteristics:

- informal AI usage,
- limited awareness,
- no structured processes.

---

# Level 2

## Adopter

AI is actively used.

Characteristics:

- multiple AI tools,
- emerging awareness,
- limited governance.

---

# Level 3

## Managed

Organization has basic AI practices.

Characteristics:

- defined rules,
- employee guidance,
- responsible usage.

---

# Level 4

## Governed

AI usage is systematically managed.

Characteristics:

- documented processes,
- oversight,
- regular review.

---

# Level 5

## Trusted AI Organization

AI is integrated responsibly.

Characteristics:

- mature governance,
- continuous improvement,
- transparent practices.
